# The Moon Is Slowly Breaking Up With Us — script (draft 1)

**Look:** flat-vector explainer, vertical 1080×1920, about 50 s, drawn in code. Lineless shapes with two-tone cel shading, soft glows, four-point sparkle stars, deep indigo space, rounded bold captions with key words in gold. **Earth and the Moon are cute characters** with simple faces: a couple slowly drifting apart.

**Why this works** (patterns from Kurzgesagt, Beyond the Observable Universe and similar Shorts):
- a relationship story told with planets as characters with feelings;
- an everyday comparison (fingernails);
- a real "how do we know" detail (mirrors on the Moon);
- a dinosaur moment;
- a twist ending.

## Notes on the writing

- **The hook is an emotion, not a fact.** "Breaking up" is relatable, and "slowly" undercuts it with a smile.
- **Every claim gets a picture a non-scientist can hold:** fingernails, mirrors and a laser, a spinning bulge, a dinosaur's calendar, an eclipse.
- **The twist is in two steps.** First a loss (no more eclipses), then a reprieve and a reframe: you happen to live in the lucky window.
- **Hedges that stay honest:**
  - "less than ten hours" (the sources range from about 2 to 10 hours);
  - "more than ten times bigger" (one simulation gives 6.25° against 0.5° today);
  - "about 600 million years" (the NASA-quoted estimate; Meeus puts the very last totality later, as eclipses fade out between about 620 million and 1.2 billion years from now).
- **Line 10** compresses two facts. Earth and the Moon would need about 50 billion years to lock face to face, and the Sun becomes a red giant in about 5 billion years, so the Moon never gets the chance to leave.
