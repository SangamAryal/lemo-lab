# FACTS — What If Earth Stopped Spinning?

| Line | Claim | Source | Hedge |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | The ground near the equator moves about 1,600 km/h (≈1,000 mph). | Earth's equatorial circumference ≈ 40,075 km ÷ 23.93 h ≈ 1,670 km/h. Space.com: "What would happen if Earth stopped spinning?" | "about", "near the equator" |
| 4–5 | A sudden stop throws anything not attached to bedrock eastward. | Smithsonian, *How Things Fly*, "Ask an Explainer": land masses would be "wiped clean of anything not attached to bedrock". Earth spins eastward, so the momentum carries things east. | "anything not stuck to solid rock" |
| 6 | The atmosphere keeps moving, faster than any tornado, across most of the planet. | Smithsonian: "the atmosphere would continue moving at 1100 mph at the equator". The strongest tornado winds (EF5) are about 480 km/h, so 1,600 km/h is several times faster. The speed falls toward the poles. | "across most of the planet" |
| 7 | The oceans keep moving and crash over the land. | Same momentum argument; Space.com and Sky at Night describe huge ocean surges. | No height given (no good source) |
| 9 | One sunrise a year: about 6 months of day and 6 months of night. | Smithsonian: "If spinning stopped completely you would get 1/2 year daylight and 1/2 year night." | — |
| 10 | The day side bakes and the night side freezes. | Follows from six-month days and nights (Space.com; ScienceNotes). | — |
| 11–12 | Spinning makes Earth bulge at the equator; without it, the water drains toward the poles, leaving one large equatorial landmass. | Witold Fraczek, Esri *ArcUser* (2010), "If the Earth Stood Still": a model showing two polar oceans and one equatorial megacontinent. Popular Science and Gizmodo covered it. This is a model that assumes the rock keeps its shape. https://www.esri.com/news/arcuser/0610/nospin.html | "would" (the scenario is a model) |
| 13 | Earth's magnetic field could weaken, letting in more radiation. | Smithsonian: Earth's magnetic field "would weaken". Live Science: the field would likely weaken but wouldn't vanish quickly. | "could weaken" |
| 14 | Nothing can stop Earth suddenly. | No known force could; the Smithsonian notes that a gradual stop wouldn't have these effects. | — |
| 15 | Earth's spin is slowing; the day gets about 2 thousandths of a second longer per century. | Tidal friction estimates run 1.5–2.4 ms per century (NASA, as quoted in news; EarthDate; tidal rhythmite studies). Day length also wobbles from year to year (ice, groundwater, the core), so it's an average trend. | "about" |
| 16 | The Moon causes this braking, and the same tides push the Moon away. | Tidal friction transfers angular momentum from Earth's spin to the Moon's orbit (EarthDate; NASA). The Moon recedes about 3.8 cm a year (our Moon video's FACTS). | — |
| 17 | Billions of years before it matters. | At ~2 ms per century, a stop by tides alone would take far longer than the Sun's remaining lifetime. | "billions of years" |
