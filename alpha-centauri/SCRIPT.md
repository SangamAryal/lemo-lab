# What If Our Sun Was Replaced by Alpha Centauri A? (script v2)

**Brief**
- **Audience:** general Shorts viewers aged 13 and up.
- **Length:** about 105 s.
- **Format:** one narrator (Andrew Multilingual, −2%, our Moon Gone voice). No talking-planet dialogue. Gen Z humour in moderation, facts accurate.
- **Look:** our flat 2.5D style.
  - Almost every beat happens on Earth's surface: a park, a city at noon, farms, forests, a reef, the poles, a coastal city.
  - Space only appears for the swap, the orbit and Mars.
- **Sound:** one SFX per second (whoosh into boom on reveals), a steady mellow music bed, no meme sounds.

**Story spine:** "What if Alpha Centauri A replaced the Sun?" → "a near-perfect replacement" → "wait, it's only a little different" → that little difference cascades through people, crops, animals, oceans and ice → the loop that doesn't stop → Mars wins → the payoff: our Sun's exact settings are why we're here.

**Assumption (shown once on screen):** the swap is instant, and Earth keeps its position and speed. Only the star changes.

## Fact checks
Unless noted, the calculations assume an instant swap with Earth at 1 AU and 29.8 km/s.

| Claim | Check | Hedge |
|---|---|---|
| It takes about 8 minutes for light, and gravity changes travel at light speed | 499 s ≈ 8 min 19 s. Gravity changes travel at c, confirmed by GW170817. | "eight minutes" |
| Alpha Centauri A is the closest star almost exactly like our Sun | G2V, the same class as the Sun. 4.37 light-years away. | "almost exactly" |
| A bit heavier, a bit bigger, the same color | 1.08 M☉, 1.22 R☉, Teff 5,790 K (Sun: 5,772 K) | "a bit" |
| 50% brighter | L = 1.52 L☉ | "fifty percent" |
| The orbit becomes a slight oval | a = M / (2M − 1) = 0.932 AU; closest point 0.864 AU; e ≈ 0.07 | "slight" |
| A year of about 10.5 months | P = √(a³/M) = 0.866 yr ≈ 316 days | "about" |
| Sunlight doubles at the closest point | 1.52 / 0.864² = 2.04× | "doubles" |
| Record heatwaves within weeks | Land responds to extra energy in days to weeks. The extra 50–100% far exceeds anything in recorded climate. | "within weeks" |
| Grids, AC and outdoor work | Follows from extreme, widespread heat | — |
| Wheat, rice and corn fail in extreme heat | Heat stress above about 35 °C at flowering sharply cuts yields | "start failing" |
| Animals move toward the poles and up mountains | This is already observed with today's warming | "many can't" |
| Coral bleaching, and hurricanes fed by warm water | Well established | — |
| Seas rise about 70 m if all the ice melts | USGS estimate about 70 m | "if all of it melts" (takes centuries or more, so no timing given) |
| The water-vapor loop could make Earth Venus-like | The runaway greenhouse threshold is about 1.1× today's sunlight; 1.5–2× is far past it | "could", "over a long time" |
| Mars gets almost as much light as Earth does today | Mars gets 0.43× Earth's sunlight now. That becomes 0.66× at the far point and 0.88× at the closest. | "at its closest", "almost" |
| The habitable zone moves outward toward Mars | The habitable-zone distance scales with √L = 1.23, so about 0.95–1.67 AU becomes about 1.17–2.06 AU. Earth (0.86–1 AU) ends up inside the inner edge, and Mars (1.52 AU) inside the new zone. | "comfy zone" |
