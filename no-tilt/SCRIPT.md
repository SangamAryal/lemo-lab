# What If Earth Stood Up Straight?

**Brief:** general Shorts viewers aged 13 and up · 98 s · one narrator (Ryan, en-GB-RyanNeural +6%) · plain friendly English, no slang, no meme SFX · designed thumbnail (poster.jpg).

**Structure:** a hook and four quests. Each has a problem, a solution and a payoff, and each payoff opens the next problem.

**Style (Earth Rings short-25d-v2):** one-line 74 px subtitles, each word popping in on its real spoken time, key words in gold · a perspective camera with a slow pan in every scene, a crossfade + push-in between scenes · natural pauses kept (squeeze.py only trims over-long gaps).

## Fact checks
| Claim | Check | Hedge |
|---|---|---|
| Earth is tilted 23 degrees | Axial tilt (obliquity) is about 23.4° | rounded to "23" |
| Closest to the Sun in January | Perihelion is in early January (about 147.1 million km); aphelion in early July (about 152.1 million km) | figures rounded |
| Seasons come from the tilt, not the distance | Standard; the distance changes sunlight by only about 7% | — |
| With no tilt, 12 hours of light and dark everywhere | At 0° obliquity every place has an equinox all year (the poles see the Sun on the horizon) | "everywhere" is simplified at the exact poles |
| Hot land pulls wet ocean air across Asia (the monsoon) | The summer monsoon is driven by land heating faster than the ocean; it follows the seasonal Sun | — |
| The monsoon waters the food of over a billion people | The South Asian monsoon supplies most of the rain for about 2 billion people | "over a billion" |
| The poles' ice only grows | With low tilt the poles get less summer sunlight; low obliquity favours ice-sheet growth (Milankovitch) | — |
| Wheat and apple trees wait for a cold winter | Winter wheat needs vernalisation; apple trees need winter chilling hours before they flower | — |
| Birds wait for longer days before they fly | Many migrating birds use day length (photoperiod) as their cue | — |
| Farming left to a few narrow strips | A projection, not a measurement | "likely" |
| A Mars-sized world hit the young Earth 4.5 billion years ago | Giant-impact hypothesis (Theia), about 4.5 billion years ago | — |
| The crash knocked Earth sideways | Thought to have helped set the tilt; still debated | "Scientists think" |
| The wreckage became the Moon | Leading explanation for the Moon's origin | — |
| The Moon's gravity holds the tilt steady | The Moon keeps Earth's tilt within about ±1.3° (Laskar et al. 1993); without it the tilt could drift much more | "steady" |
| "A crash from four billion years ago" | The impact was about 4.5 billion years ago, so "four billion" is a round lower figure | spoken as a round number |
