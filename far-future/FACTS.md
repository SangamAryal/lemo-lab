# FACTS — "The Next 100 Billion Years"

| # | Claim | Source | Hedge used |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | In 1,000 years, today's speech would sound as foreign as Old English does now. | Old English (about 1,000 years ago) is unintelligible to modern readers without study. Projecting the same rate of change forward is an analogy, not a measurement. | "would sound", "probably" |
| 1 | The North Star drifts off the pole. | Wikipedia, *Pole star*: by about AD 3000 the pole sits midway between Polaris and Gamma Cephei (Errai), which is closest around AD 4200. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pole_star | "starts drifting" |
| 2 | Vega becomes the North Star. | Wikipedia, *Pole star*: around AD 14,000, Vega is the pole star, within about 4–5° of the pole. That's about 12,000 years from now. | "give or take" |
| 3 | Betelgeuse has probably exploded. | Estimates put Betelgeuse's supernova about 100,000 years away, though that depends on its mass; at ~15 solar masses it could be a million years or more (Discover; Syfy Wire summarising the research). | "probably" |
| 4 | A passing star crosses the outer solar system and disturbs comets. | Gliese 710 passes about 10,600 au (0.05 pc) from the Sun in about 1.29 million years, inside the inner Oort cloud (Gaia DR3 analysis, 2022; ESA). It could send comets inward over the following millions of years. https://www.esa.int/Our_Activities/Space_Science/Close_encounters_of_the_stellar_kind | "knocks comets loose" |
| 5 | Africa may split, with a new ocean. | The East African Rift separates the Nubian and Somali plates; the Gulf of Aden and Red Sea are expected to eventually flood the Afar region and the rift valley (Ken Macdonald, UC Santa Barbara). The commonly quoted timing is 5–10 million years, but I couldn't confirm that from a primary source, so the line says "may be splitting". | "may be" |
| 6 | Phobos breaks up into a ring around Mars. | Black & Mittal (2015), *Nature Geoscience*: Phobos will be pulled apart by Mars's tides in about 20–40 million years, forming a ring. | "thirty million years" (middle of the range) |
| 7 | Saturn's rings may be gone within 100 million years. | O'Donoghue et al. 2018 (NASA Goddard, *Icarus*): with ring rain plus the infall Cassini measured, "the rings have less than 100 million years to live" (about 300 million years on ring rain alone). The Mars ring could last up to about 100 million years. | "may have" |
| 8 | The continents merge into a new supercontinent in about 250 million years. | Scotese's Pangaea Proxima / Ultima model: a supercontinent within about 250 million years (Wikipedia; NASA APOD 2000-10-02). | — |
| 9 | In about 1 billion years the Sun is about 10% brighter and the oceans begin to evaporate. | Schröder & Smith (2008), "Distant future of the Sun and Earth revisited": about a billion years until a 10% rise in solar flux, when "the oceans will start to evaporate". https://arxiv.org/abs/0801.4031 | "start to" |
| 10 | The Sun becomes a red giant in about 5 billion years. | Wikipedia, *Sun*: core hydrogen fusion declines in about 4–7 billion years and the Sun swells into a red giant. | — |
| 10 | Andromeda and the Milky Way have about a 50/50 chance of merging. | Sawala et al. 2025, *Nature Astronomy* (Hubble + Gaia, 100,000 simulations): about 50% chance of a merger within 10 billion years and under 2% within 5 billion years. If it happens, likely in 7–8 billion years (Durham University; Sky & Telescope). | "might", "coin flip" |
| 11 | In about 10 billion years the Sun is a white dwarf about Earth's size. | Standard stellar evolution: after the red giant phase, the Sun leaves an Earth-sized white dwarf (Wikipedia, *Sun*; *White dwarf*). | "about" |
| 12 | In about 100 billion years, galaxies beyond the Local Group have left our view; observers would see one merged galaxy and lose the evidence for the Big Bang. | Krauss & Scherrer 2007, "The Return of a Static Universe and the End of Cosmology". Loeb 2011 (Harvard CfA): "in 100 billion years, all galaxies outside the Local Group… will have sped out beyond the event horizon"; the Local Group merges into "Milkomeda". Krauss and Scherrer give the full loss of evidence over trillions of years, so the line only says observers "would think their galaxy is the whole universe". https://www.cfa.harvard.edu/news/far-future-astronomers-could-still-deduce-big-bang | "would think" |

## Checked and left out
- **"Gliese 710 passes at 0.05 light years".** The current figure is 0.05 parsecs (about 0.17 light years); some popular articles mix up the units. The name only appears on screen, not in the narration.
- **"An asteroid like the dinosaur-killer every 100 million years".** I couldn't confirm the frequency from a good source, so it isn't used.
- **"Andromeda will collide in 4.5 billion years".** That's the old prediction; the 2025 study made it a coin flip, and the script says so.
