# Talking Planets #2: "What If Another Star Replaced the Sun?" (script v1)

**1. Brief**
- **Audience:** general Shorts viewers aged 13 and up.
- **Length:** about 42 s.
- **Tone:** a game show. The Sun takes a day off and sends replacements, and Earth reacts to each one. Simple spoken English, a few Gen Z words, accurate facts.
- **Voices:** no narrator. Each character has its own edge-tts voice, at about 2.5 words/s with tight gaps.
- **Different from the reference channel:**
  - Earth's point of view, with a quick ground shot for each star.
  - A mix of small and big stars, each teaching a different idea: dim light, weak gravity, too much light, too big.
  - A wholesome ending, not a scary one.

## 3. Timing
- **About 88 spoken words** in 15 short lines.
- That's about 35 s of speech at 2.5 words/s, plus quick reactions and the rewind beat, for **about 42 s** in total.
- Within budget.
- If it runs long, cut line 12–13 (the explode joke). The story still works without it.

**Gen Z words** (kept light): bro · literally · no cap · or nah · Byeee.

## 4. Fact checks

All scenarios assume the star swaps in instantly at the Sun's spot, with Earth at its usual distance and speed.

| Claim | Check | Hedge |
|---|---|---|
| Sirius B is a white dwarf about Earth's size | Diameter about 12,000 km; Earth is 12,742 km | "you're my size?" (as a question) |
| Sirius B pulls like the Sun but is dim, so Earth freezes | Mass about 1.0 Sun, so Earth's orbit stays about the same. It gives out only a few % of the Sun's energy, which makes Earth's temperature about −150 °C. | "so dim" |
| Proxima is the closest star to us | 4.24 light-years; the closest star after the Sun (NASA) | — |
| Proxima is too light to hold Earth | Proxima's mass is about 0.12 Sun. Earth moves at about 30 km/s, but would only need about 15 km/s to escape, so Earth flies off. | — |
| Sirius A is 25× brighter than the Sun | Luminosity about 25 L☉ | — |
| Earth's oceans would boil | 25× the sunlight is far past the runaway greenhouse limit (~1.1×) | "boiling" (as a reaction line) |
| Betelgeuse would reach past Mars | Radius estimates are about 640–1,000 Suns, or 3–4.7 AU. Mars orbits at 1.52 AU, so Earth (1 AU) would be inside the star. | "past Mars" (the lowest estimate still clears it) |
| Betelgeuse will explode someday | It's expected to go supernova within about 100,000 years | "Someday" (no date) |

## 5. Notes
- No star "destroys" the solar system in a countdown, and there's no black-hole ending. This keeps us clearly different from the reference video.
- Ground shots for 4 of the stars (frozen street, Earth flung away, boiling beach, red sky) are our "show it where it happens" signature.
- Thumbnail idea: Earth sweating and tiny on the left, a giant Betelgeuse filling the right. Headline: **"WHAT IF THIS REPLACED OUR SUN?"**
