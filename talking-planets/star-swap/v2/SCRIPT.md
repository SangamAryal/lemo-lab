# Talking Planets #2: "What If Another Star Replaced the Sun?" (script v2)

**What changed from v1:** v1 only had reactions ("I'm freezing", "my oceans are boiling") with no explanation.

In v2, each star covers the same five things:
1. **Gravity and orbit:** what its pull does to Earth's path.
2. **Year and seasons:** how long a year lasts, and what the seasons become.
3. **The sky from the ground:** what you'd actually see.
4. **The ground and people:** temperature, oceans, where life could hang on.
5. **The other planets:** Mars, Jupiter and Neptune react.

To fit that in ~100 s, there are **3 stars**, each failing in a different way:
- **Proxima:** too light.
- **Sirius A:** too heavy and too bright.
- **Betelgeuse:** far too big.

The ending ties them together: the Sun is "just right". Sirius B was cut for length.

**Brief**
- **Audience:** general Shorts viewers aged 13 and up.
- **Length:** about 100 s (under the 120 s cap).
- **Tone:** simple spoken English. Earth explains, the others react briefly.
- **Voices:** Earth = Andrew, Sun = Brian, Proxima = Jenny, Sirius A = Eric, Betelgeuse = Guy (deep), Mars = Christopher, Jupiter = Guy (−10Hz), Neptune = Steffan.

**On-screen structure:** a progress bar at the top, "STAR 1 / 3". For each star, 4 chips light up as they're covered: **ORBIT · SKY · GROUND · PLANETS**. Viewers can see each star is examined the same way.

**Rule of the game (said once, line 2):** the swap is instant, and every planet keeps its position and speed.

## Fact checks
All of these assume an instant swap at the Sun's position, with each planet keeping its current distance and speed. My calculations follow from those starting conditions.

| Claim | Check | Hedge |
|---|---|---|
| Proxima is the closest star to us | 4.24 light-years (NASA) | — |
| Proxima is 1/8 of the Sun's mass | 0.122 M☉ | "only an eighth" |
| Proxima can't hold any planet | A planet in a circular orbit flies off if the new mass is under ½ M☉ (its speed is 2.0× the new circular speed). This holds for every planet at any distance. | — |
| A dim red dot from the ground | Radius 0.154 R☉, so about 1/6 of the Sun's width at 1 AU. Visible light is about 1/17,000 of sunlight. It shrinks as Earth flies away. | "dim red dot" |
| Below freezing within a week, about −73 °C within a year | The standard "Sun switched off" estimate (D. Stevenson, Caltech, in Scientific American). Proxima adds only 0.17 % of the Sun's energy. | "within a week", "about minus seventy" |
| Warm spots only near volcanoes and deep underground | Geothermal heat and hydrothermal vents | "warm spots" (no promise that people survive) |
| Sirius A: 2 suns of mass, 25× brighter | 2.06 M☉, 25.4 L☉ | — |
| Earth's orbit becomes an oval, diving closer than Mercury | With M = 2.06, Earth at 1 AU and 29.8 km/s is at the far end of a new orbit: a = 0.66 AU, closest point 0.32 AU (Mercury orbits at 0.39 AU) | — |
| A year of about 4.5 months | Period = √(a³/M) = √(0.66³/2.06) = 0.374 yr ≈ 136 days | "about" |
| 10× more light at the close end | (1 / 0.32)² ≈ 9.8 | "ten times" |
| Nearly twice as wide as the Sun from the ground | 1.71 R☉ at 1 AU | "nearly twice" |
| The oceans boil away | 25× (up to about 250× at the close end) is far past the runaway greenhouse threshold (about 1.1×) | — |
| Jupiter "finally feels warm", and its icy moons could melt | At 5.2 AU, Jupiter gets 25/27 ≈ 0.9× Earth's current sunlight, and up to about 9× at the close end of its new oval | "could" |
| Betelgeuse reaches past Mars | Radius about 764 R☉ ≈ 3.55 AU (Joyce et al. 2020; estimates range from about 640 to 1,000 R☉). Mars orbits at 1.52 AU. | "past Mars" (the lowest estimate still clears it) |
| About 18 solar masses | 16.5–19 M☉ (Joyce et al. 2020) | "about eighteen" |
| Even Neptune gets dragged in | With M ≈ 18, a planet's closest point falls to about 3 % of its current distance. Neptune's (30 AU) becomes about 0.9 AU, deep inside the star. This holds across the 16.5–19 range. | — |
| Neptune is 30× farther out than Earth | 30.07 AU | — |
| A red sun 25× wider from Neptune | 2 × 3.55 / 30.07 rad ≈ 13.5°, against the Sun's 0.53° from Earth, so about 25× (about 21× with the lowest radius estimate) | "twenty-five times" (could say "over twenty") |
