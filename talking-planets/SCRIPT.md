# Talking Planets #1: "Who Has the Longest Day?" (test script v1)

**1. Brief**
- **Audience:** general Shorts viewers aged 13 and up.
- **Length:** about 40 s, a test of the shorter format.
- **Tone:** sitcom-style banter between the planets, with Gen Z slang in moderation and accurate facts.
- **Voices:** no narrator. Each planet has its own edge-tts voice (cast below). Dialogue pace is about 2.5 words/s, plus short reaction gaps.
- **Format, from the reference analysis:**
  - The question stays on screen the whole time.
  - The planets are revealed in order from shortest to longest day, with a progress HUD.
  - One number per beat.
  - A twist at the end, then a comment prompt shown as text only.

## 3. Timing
- **About 97 spoken words** in 17 short lines.
- That's about 39 s of speech at 2.5 words/s, plus around 0.3 s between speakers and the "WHAT" beat, for **about 44 s** in total.
- Within the ~40–45 s budget.
- If it runs long, cut line 8 ("Mercury ate") or line 13 ("No cap?").

**Gen Z slang used** (6, so it doesn't feel forced): bro · it's giving · mid · ate · no cap · main character. Also the "That's illegal" joke.

## 4. Fact checks

| Claim | Source / check | Hedge |
|---|---|---|
| Jupiter spins in under 10 hours | Jupiter's rotation period is about 9 h 56 min (NASA Planetary Fact Sheet) | "under ten hours" |
| Earth's day is about 24 hours | Solar day 24 h; one spin (sidereal) 23 h 56 min | "about" |
| Mars's day is about 40 minutes longer | Mars spins in 24 h 37 min, about 41 min more than Earth's 23 h 56 min | "forty minutes" (rounded) |
| Mercury spins once in 59 days | Rotation period 58.6 Earth days (NASA) | rounded to 59 |
| Venus spins once in 243 days | Rotation period 243.0 Earth days, the slowest of any planet (NASA) | — |
| Venus's year is 225 days | Orbital period 224.7 Earth days | "two twenty-five" |
| Venus's day is longer than its year | 243 > 224.7, comparing one spin (sidereal day) with one orbit | True for "1 day = 1 spin", which is why that definition stays on screen. Venus's sunrise-to-sunrise day is about 117 days, so it only works with the spin definition. |
| Venus spins backwards and the Sun rises in the west | Retrograde rotation (NASA) | You couldn't actually see the Sun through Venus's thick clouds, so the ground shot shows it dim. |

**Why "1 day = 1 spin" matters:** Mercury's sunrise-to-sunrise day is about 176 Earth days, longer than Venus's 117. Keeping "1 day = 1 spin" pinned on screen keeps every number true.
