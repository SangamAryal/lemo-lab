# The Sun Is Lazy — v2 (story-first, about 50 s)

**Title:** "The Sun Is Terrible at Its Job". **First frame (no sound):** the Sun lounging in a deck chair under huge text, **"THE SUN IS BAD AT ITS JOB"**.
**What changed from v1 (75 s → 50 s):** it opens on a contradiction instead of "the most powerful thing in our sky". The photon section is gone (a good fact, but it answers no question in this story). New escalation: the probability visual and the "hundred trillion trillion trillion per second" reveal. The payoff now reframes the hook.

| Beat | Line | Visual (pattern interrupts in **bold**) | Question it leaves |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hook 0–2 s | The Sun is terrible at its job. | The Sun on its deck chair sips from its mug and slowly lowers its sunglasses, offended. | Terrible? How? |
| Mystery | Its core is fifteen million degrees. And one cubic metre of it makes about as much heat as a compost heap. | A thermometer shoots off the top, then **a hard cut** to the glowing cube beside a steaming compost heap, both reading ~280 W. A fly lands. | Really that weak? |
| Mystery | You make more heat than that. Per cubic metre, you are out-performing a star. | The couch scroller glows orange; "EMPLOYEE OF THE MONTH". The Sun peeks in, annoyed. | Then how does it light anything? |
| Question | So how does a compost heap light up the whole solar system? | The compost heap with an extension cord to a flickering Earth bulb. **0.3 s of silence.** | |
| Explanation | Fusion. But fusion is hard. Protons are all positive, so they shove each other away. | **An extreme zoom** into the core; two protons bump and bounce apart. | How do they ever fuse? |
| Twist | The only way through is to cheat. Quantum tunnelling: walking through the wall anyway. | One proton ghosts through a wall labelled "THE RULES". | How often does that work? |
| Escalation | It's so unlikely, the average proton waits about nine billion years for its turn. | A waiting room, ticket #9,000,000,000, a cobweb growing. | So how is there any sunlight? |
| Scale | But the Sun has so many protons, it still pulls this off about a hundred trillion trillion trillion times a second. | **Probability made visible:** 100 proton pairs try and 99 fail, one ✨tunnels✨. Then a zoom out to millions, then the whole core sparkling, as a counter reads 10³⁸ /s. | Why does slowness matter? |
| Payoff | And that slowness is the secret. If fusion were easy, the Sun would have burned out before life ever began. | A "keen" Sun flares and fizzles; Earth goes grey and a fish declines to leave the sea. Rewind to today: oceans, dinosaurs, you. | |
| Reframe | So the Sun isn't lazy. It's pacing itself. | The Sun pushes its sunglasses back up and winks. It loops straight into the opening frame. | |

**Facts:** as in ../FACTS.md, plus about 9×10³⁷ pp reactions per second (NASA StarChild: Sun luminosity ÷ energy per reaction), said as "about a hundred trillion trillion trillion" (10³⁸). Fusion with "basically never" became "it's hard" plus tunnelling; the nine-billion-year wait is phrased as "the average proton waits", never "takes 9 billion years to fuse".
