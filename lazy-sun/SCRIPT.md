# "The Sun Is Lazy (And That's Why You Exist)" — script, draft 1

**Channel:** Beyond Everything. **Style:** flat-vector, same look as the Moon film: cute characters with cel shading, sparkle stars, gold keyword captions. Vertical 1080×1920, about 70 s.

**Characters:**
- **Sun:** big and round. Half-closed eyes, sunglasses pushed up, and a mug of something hot. Never in a hurry.
- **Earth:** the same Earth character as in the Moon film.
- **Photon:** a tiny yellow spark that pinballs around.
- **Two protons:** small red balls that can't stand each other.

**Humour:** the narrator plays it straight, as if reading a performance review of a star. The jokes come from understatement and from the visuals contradicting the grand words. No meme sounds, no "bruh".

| # | Narration | On screen |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The Sun is the most powerful thing in our sky. | Epic push-in on a blazing Sun, choir-ish swell, lens flare. |
| 2 | It is also, technically… lazy. | Music cuts. Reverse angle: the Sun is lounging on a deck chair in space, sipping from a mug. It slowly lowers its sunglasses. |
| 3 | Take one cubic metre from the very centre of its core. | A glowing cube is lifted out of a cut-away Sun like a slice of cake. A thermometer bursts its top. |
| 4 | It puts out about as much heat as… a compost heap. | The cube sits next to a steaming garden compost heap; matching wattmeters read about 280 W. A fly lands on the cube. |
| 5 | Your body makes more heat per cubic metre than that. Several times more. | A person on a couch holding a phone, glowing orange; their meter swings past the Sun's: "≈1,300 W/m³". The Sun stares at them. |
| 6 | Congratulations. You are out-performing a star. Please don't put it on your CV. | Gold "★ EMPLOYEE OF THE MONTH" frame around the person. The Sun takes a slow, offended sip. |
| 7 | So how does a compost heap light up the solar system? It's just… very, very big. | Zoom out: the cube becomes one of countless cubes, then the whole core. Earth appears beside it as a speck. |
| 8 | The light warming your face today was made in the core thousands of years ago. | The Photon is born in the core and pinballs off atoms. A calendar flips through centuries and the walls build up: pyramids, castles, a smartphone. |
| 9 | It spent all that time bumping around inside, then crossed to Earth in eight minutes, like it suddenly remembered it had somewhere to be. | The Photon pops out of the surface, checks a tiny wristwatch, and zips off to Earth in a streak. It lands on a sunbather's nose. |
| 10 | Even fusion is a struggle. Protons push each other away. By normal physics, they should basically never touch. | Two red Protons back away from each other, arms crossed. |
| 11 | They only fuse thanks to quantum tunnelling. Which is physics for: walking through the wall anyway. | One Proton ghosts straight through a brick wall labelled "THE RULES". |
| 12 | Even then, the average proton waits about nine billion years for its turn. | A waiting room; a Proton holds ticket #9,000,000,000. A clock spins, a cobweb grows on the chair. |
| 13 | Here's the twist. If fusion were easy, the Sun would have burned out long before anything crawled out of the sea. | A "keen" Sun flares fast and fizzles out; the Earth below turns grey. A fish on the beach shrugs and slides back into the water. |
| 14 | Instead, it took its time. Long enough for oceans… dinosaurs… and you. | A timeline sweep: oceans, the first cells, a dinosaur waving, then the person on the couch, who waves back. |
| 15 | So the next time someone calls you lazy, just tell them you're pacing yourself. Like a star. | The Sun and the person, side by side on deck chairs with mugs. The Sun lifts its sunglasses and winks. Sparkle, then cut to black. |

**Length:** about 200 words, about 70 s as recorded (65 s of speech).

**Why it should hold attention:**
- **The hook flips in the first 3 seconds:** "most powerful" turns into "lazy".
- **The middle beats each have a visual joke:** compost heap, employee of the month, the photon's wristwatch, the waiting-room ticket.
- **The ending pays off the joke and the science together:** the slowness is why life had time.
- **The last line loops back to the first,** so viewers can rewatch it.
