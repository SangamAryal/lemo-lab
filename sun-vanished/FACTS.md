# FACTS — What If the Sun Vanished?

| Line | Claim | Source | Hedge |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1–2 | Sunlight takes about 8 minutes (8 min 20 s, ≈ 499 s) to reach Earth. | 1 AU ≈ 149.6 million km ÷ 299,792 km/s ≈ 499 s. NASA Space Place, "How long does it take sunlight to reach Earth?" | "about eight minutes" |
| 3 | Earth would keep orbiting for those ~8 minutes, because changes in gravity also travel at the speed of light. | General relativity; confirmed by GW170817 (2017), where gravitational waves and light from the same event arrived within ~2 s after 130 million years (LIGO/Virgo, *ApJL* 848 L13). | — |
| 5 | Earth would fly off in a straight line at about 30 km/s. | Earth's mean orbital speed is 29.8 km/s ≈ 107,000 km/h (NASA Earth Fact Sheet). With no Sun, it carries on along the tangent (Newton's first law). | "about", "straight line" (ignoring the planets' small pulls) |
| 6 | The Moon stays with Earth. | The Moon is bound to Earth (it orbits well inside Earth's Hill sphere, ~1.5 million km). | — |
| 7 | The Moon goes dark at about the same moment; Jupiter keeps shining for over an hour. | Moonlight is reflected sunlight, and the Moon is about the same distance from the Sun as we are. Sunlight takes ~43 min to reach Jupiter, then 35–55 min to reach us, so Jupiter keeps shining ~70–90 min after the Sun goes dark for us. | "over an hour" |
| 8 | Photosynthesis stops. | Plants need light to photosynthesize. Scientific American (2007), "What would happen to Earth if the Sun disappeared?" (David Stevenson, Caltech): photosynthesis would stop immediately. | — |
| 9 | Within a week, the average surface temperature falls to about −18 °C (0 °F). | Scientific American (2007), Stevenson: "within a week, the average global surface temperature would drop below 0 degrees Fahrenheit". | "about", "on average" |
| 10 | Within a year, about −70 °C (−73 °C / −100 °F); the oceans freeze over. | Same source: "within a year, it would dip to −100 degrees F"; the oceans' surfaces would freeze. | "about" |
| 11 | The ice insulates; the deep ocean stays liquid for a long time, warmed by Earth's internal heat. | Same source: the ice would insulate the water below, and the oceans would not freeze solid for hundreds of thousands of years. | No figure given in the script |
| 12 | Life at hydrothermal vents doesn't depend on sunlight. | Vent ecosystems run on chemosynthesis (NOAA Ocean Exploration, "What is a hydrothermal vent?"). Stevenson notes such life could survive. | — |
| 13 | The Sun can't just vanish; its mass (about 333,000 Earths) has to go somewhere. | Conservation of mass-energy. Sun's mass: NASA Sun Fact Sheet (1.989 × 10³⁰ kg, 333,000 Earth masses). | "about" |
| 14 | The sunlight you see left the Sun about 8 minutes ago. | Same as line 1. | "eight minutes" |
