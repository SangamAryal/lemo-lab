# What If the Asteroid Had Missed Earth? (script v1)

**Brief:** general Shorts viewers aged 13 and up · about 75 s · one narrator (Ryan, +5%) · plain friendly English, no slang · one-line captions · designed thumbnail.

| # | Narration | Visual / point of view |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | You're alive because a rock hit exactly the wrong spot. | **GROUND:** a person looks at their phone, then the scene rewinds fast through time. |
| 2 | 66 million years ago, an asteroid about 10 kilometres wide slammed into what is now Mexico. | **GROUND:** dinosaurs graze by a shallow sea as a bright streak crosses the sky. Impact flash. |
| 3 | The seabed there was packed with sulfur and oil-rich rock. The impact threw it all into the sky. | **CUTAWAY:** the rock layers under the sea, blasted upward into a black plume. |
| 4 | Soot and sulfur blocked the Sun for months. Plants died. Then the animals that ate them. | **GROUND:** the sky goes dark, ferns turn brown, a plant-eater collapses, then a hunter. |
| 5 | About three out of every four species disappeared, including almost every dinosaur. | A grid of animal icons: three in every four fade out. |
| 6 | But here's the strange part. One study found only about 13 percent of Earth's surface would have been that deadly. | **SPACE:** a globe with small red patches. The asteroid's path rewinds. Card: **ONLY ~13%**. |
| 7 | Almost anywhere else, and the dinosaurs may have made it. | The asteroid lands in open ocean. Dinosaurs look up, then carry on eating. |
| 8 | So what if it had missed completely? | **SPACE:** the asteroid passes Earth and flies on. |
| 9 | Dinosaurs had already ruled for about 165 million years. There was no reason for them to stop. | **GROUND:** a thriving forest full of dinosaurs. Counter: **165 MILLION YEARS**. |
| 10 | And our ancestors? Small, nervous mammals, most no bigger than a badger, hiding from anything with teeth. | **GROUND, night:** a tiny mammal peeks from a burrow as a huge foot lands beside it. |
| 11 | When the dinosaurs vanished, mammals finally got their chance. Within a million years, they were growing much bigger. | The mammal steps outside. It grows bigger across quick time jumps. |
| 12 | No asteroid, and that chance may never come. No apes. No cities. No you. | **GROUND:** apes, a city and the person from line 1 each fade away. A dinosaur walks through the empty spot. |
| 13 | Would something else have become smart instead? Nobody knows. | A dinosaur tilts its head and looks at the camera. A big question mark. |
| 14 | But here's what we do know. Not every dinosaur died. A few small feathered ones survived. | **GROUND:** after the darkness, a small feathered dinosaur flies out of the ash. |
| 15 | So look outside. That pigeon on your window is a dinosaur. | **GROUND, today:** the first person's window, with a pigeon on the ledge. Its shadow is a dinosaur's. |
| 16 | And it survived the same rock that made you possible. | The pigeon and the person look at each other. On-screen text: **"What would Earth look like today? ↓"** |

## Fact checks
| Claim | Check | Hedge |
|---|---|---|
| 66 million years ago, about 10 km wide, in Mexico | Chicxulub impact, Yucatán; estimates about 10–15 km | "about" |
| Sulfur and oil-rich rock | The target rock held sulfate and hydrocarbons (Kaiho & Oshima 2017) | — |
| Soot and sulfur blocked the Sun for months | Models give months to about two years of darkness or cooling | "for months" |
| About three in four species disappeared | About 75% of species | "about" |
| Only about 13% of the surface would have been that deadly | Kaiho & Oshima 2017, Scientific Reports. One model, so it's credited as "one study". | "one study", "about" |
| Dinosaurs may have survived elsewhere | The same study's implication | "may have" |
| Ruled for about 165 million years | About 230 to 66 million years ago | "about" |
| Most mammals no bigger than a badger | Most Cretaceous mammals were small; a few were larger | "most" |
| Mammals much bigger within a million years | Corral Bluffs fossils show large mammals within about 700,000 years | "within a million years" |
| Birds are surviving dinosaurs | Birds are living theropod dinosaurs | — |
| Whether another smart species would appear | Unknown | "Nobody knows" |
