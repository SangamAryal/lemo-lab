# What If the Asteroid Had Missed Earth? (script v2: quest structure)

**Brief:** general Shorts viewers aged 13 and up · about 90 s · one narrator (Ryan, +5%) · plain friendly English, no slang · designed thumbnail.

**Structure:** four quests. Each has a problem, a solution and a payoff, and each payoff opens the next problem.

**Style (from Earth Rings short-25d-v2):**
- Subtitles: big bold words (74 px, navy outline, no panel), about 18 characters at a time, each word popping in on its real spoken time, key words in gold.
- Camera: a real perspective camera with a slow pan in every scene, a crossfade with a push-in between scenes, and a slow drift inside each shot. No pinned title bar.

| # | Quest | Narration | Visual / point of view |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Hook | You're alive because a rock landed in exactly the wrong spot. | **GROUND, today:** a person at a window, then the world rewinds fast. |
| 2 | Q1 | 66 million years ago, an asteroid about 10 kilometres wide hit what is now Mexico. | **GROUND:** dinosaurs by a shallow sea, a bright streak in the sky, the flash. |
| 3 | Q1 problem | But here's the problem. On a classroom globe, that rock would be a grain of sand. How does a grain of sand kill the dinosaurs? | **SPACE:** the camera pans around a globe, with a tiny speck beside it. |
| 4 | Q1 solution | The answer was under the sea. The seabed was packed with sulfur and oil-rich rock, and the impact threw it into the sky. | **CUTAWAY:** the camera dives through the rock layers, then a black plume rises. |
| 5 | Q1 payoff | Soot and sulfur blocked the Sun for months. One study found only about 13 percent of Earth's surface would have been that deadly. | **SPACE:** a dark veil wraps the globe, with red patches. Card: **ONLY ~13%**. |
| 6 | Q1 payoff | It didn't just hit Earth. It hit one of the worst places it could. | The camera pushes in on the impact spot. |
| 7 | Q2 problem | Now imagine you're alive in that darkness. No sunlight. Plants are dying. What do you eat? | **GROUND:** a dark forest, ferns browning, ash falling. |
| 8 | Q2 | Big animals starved first. Almost nothing on land bigger than a large dog made it. | **GROUND:** huge dinosaurs collapse in silhouette. Size bar: **DOG-SIZED**. |
| 9 | Q2 solution | The survivors were small. They hid in burrows and lived on seeds, insects and rotting plants. | **UNDERGROUND:** a cutaway burrow, the camera sliding along the tunnel. |
| 10 | Q2 payoff | And one of those small survivors was our ancestor. | The tiny mammal turns to the camera. |
| 11 | Q3 problem | So what if the asteroid had missed? | **SPACE:** the asteroid sails past Earth. |
| 12 | Q3 problem | Mammals had already lived beside dinosaurs for over 100 million years, and most never got bigger than a badger. Every big job on land was taken. | **GROUND, night:** a mammal peeks out as a huge foot lands beside it. Cards fill: **HUNTER · GRAZER · GIANT**, all taken. |
| 13 | Q3 solution | When the dinosaurs vanished, those jobs opened up. Within a million years, mammals were growing much bigger. | **GROUND:** the mammal walks out into the open and grows over time jumps. |
| 14 | Q3 payoff | No asteroid, and that door may never open. No apes. No cities. No you. | Apes, a city and the person each fade away. A dinosaur walks through. |
| 15 | Q4 problem | One last thing. Everyone says the dinosaurs died out. But did they? | **GROUND:** a fossil in the ash, then the camera rises. |
| 16 | Q4 solution | A few small feathered ones made it through the dark. | **GROUND:** a small feathered dinosaur flies out of the ash into the light. |
| 17 | Q4 payoff | So look outside. That pigeon on your window is a dinosaur. And it survived the same rock that made you possible. | **GROUND, today:** back to the first window. A pigeon on the ledge casts a dinosaur's shadow. On-screen text: **"What would Earth look like today? ↓"** |

## Fact checks
| Claim | Check | Hedge |
|---|---|---|
| 66 million years ago, about 10 km wide, in Mexico | Chicxulub impact, Yucatán; estimates about 10–15 km | "about" |
| A grain of sand on a classroom globe | 10 km / 12,742 km on a 30 cm globe ≈ 0.24 mm, a fine sand grain | — |
| Sulfur and oil-rich rock | The target rock held sulfate and hydrocarbons (Kaiho & Oshima 2017) | — |
| Soot and sulfur blocked the Sun for months | Models give months to about two years of darkness or cooling | "for months" |
| Only about 13% of the surface would have been that deadly | Kaiho & Oshima 2017, Scientific Reports. One model, so credited as "one study". | "one study", "about", "one of the worst" |
| Almost nothing on land bigger than a large dog survived | Commonly cited limit of about 25 kg on land, with a few exceptions such as crocodilians at the water's edge | "almost nothing" |
| Survivors were small, in burrows, eating seeds, insects and rotting plants | Burrowing and detritus-based food chains are the leading explanations | — |
| Our ancestor was among the survivors | Mammal lines leading to us crossed the boundary | — |
| Mammals lived beside dinosaurs for over 100 million years | Early mammals appear over 200 million years ago | "over" |
| Most never got bigger than a badger | Most Cretaceous mammals were small; a few were larger | "most" |
| Mammals much bigger within a million years | Corral Bluffs fossils show large mammals within about 700,000 years | "within a million years" |
| Birds are surviving dinosaurs | Birds are living theropod dinosaurs | — |
