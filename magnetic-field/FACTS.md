# Facts — What If Earth Lost Its Magnetic Field?

| Claim (as said or shown) | Basis | Hedge |
|---|---|---|
| A compass would stop pointing north | A compass aligns with the global (dipole) field; without it only weak, local crustal magnetism is left. | "stop pointing north", not "spin" |
| The field comes from liquid iron swirling in the core, ~3,000 km down | Geodynamo in the liquid iron–nickel outer core, whose top is ~2,890 km deep (USGS, NASA). | "about" |
| Outer core "hotter than lava" (on-screen) | Outer core ~4,000–5,000+ °C; surface lava ~700–1,200 °C. | — |
| Solar wind ~400 km/s | Slow solar wind ~300–500 km/s, fast wind up to ~800 km/s (NASA). | "about" |
| The field bends the wind around us | The magnetosphere deflects most solar-wind plasma around Earth (NASA, ESA). | — |
| Satellites and astronauts would get more radiation | Most satellites and the ISS orbit inside the magnetosphere, which shields them from much of the solar and cosmic particle flux. | "a lot more", no number |
| Sea turtles, salmon and some birds navigate with the field | Magnetoreception: loggerhead turtles (Lohmann), Pacific salmon (Putman et al. 2013/2014), migratory songbirds. | "some birds" |
| On the ground you'd be mostly fine; the air is a second shield | The atmosphere (~10 t of air above every m², ≈ 10 m of water) absorbs most particle radiation before it reaches the ground. | "mostly" |
| The solar wind would strip the upper atmosphere bit by bit | Ion pick-up and sputtering by the solar wind on unmagnetised planets (MAVEN at Mars). Debated how much the field protects Earth (Venus keeps a thick atmosphere without one). | "would", "upper", "bit by bit" |
| Mars' field died billions of years ago and it lost most of its air | Mars' global dynamo stopped ~4 billion years ago; MAVEN found most of its early atmosphere was lost to space. | "billions of years ago", "most" |
| That took hundreds of millions of years | MAVEN-era estimates put most of Mars' loss over hundreds of millions to ~1 billion years. | "hundreds of millions" |
| Earth's stronger gravity holds on tighter | Earth's surface gravity is ~2.6× Mars'; escape velocity 11.2 vs 5.0 km/s. | — |
| 41,000 years ago the field dropped below a tenth of today's | Laschamp excursion, ~41,000 years ago; field strength fell to roughly 5–10 % of today's (Laj et al.; Cooper et al. 2021). | "below a tenth" |
| Life carried on | No mass extinction tied to the Laschamp excursion or to reversals. | — |
| The poles have swapped hundreds of times; last time 780,000 years ago (on-screen) | Hundreds of reversals in the geologic record (≈ 183 in the last 83 million years); last full reversal Brunhes–Matuyama ~780,000 years ago. | "hundreds" |

Not claimed: that auroras would appear everywhere, that GPS or power grids would fail, or any survival timeline.
